What One Seat Includes

Each line below runs today inside a working firm.

Your own wrapper

Your pleadings come out in your firm's own wrapper. Caption, format, headers, signature block, matched to your existing filings during onboarding. Not an AI drafter. Your lawyers review, sign, and file.

Every email, read twice

Every incoming email is read within minutes by two desks: one routes it to the matter and drafts the reply, the other hunts it for deadlines and court dates. Nothing is sent and nothing is calendared without a person confirming.

Deadlines, desk by desk

Every deadline is found in the mail and the record, computed from the governing rule with the earlier date always shown, confirmed by a person, and watched by a desk that wakes whoever owns it.

Urgent, your way

An urgent email reaches the people on that matter, each the way they asked to be reached: Teams, email, or the build's own alert chat.

Ask the record

One desk keeps the record: every matter, every folder, every document on the firm's drive. Ask it what the file holds, what is missing, and what should be filed next, and it answers from the documents, not from memory.

The discovery engine

The discovery engine alone pays for the build. It takes a folder and the requests and hands your staff a redacted, privilege-gated set to adopt. What you receive is read page by page against your requests: what is missing, what contradicts the record, what admits something. You decide.

A closed universe of law

Law from official sources, growing nightly without a cap, so your agents draft from what is on the shelf, never the open internet. Every opinion of the Supreme Court of the United States, from 1759 to the current term, kept current every night.

Expert witnesses

The build searches published opinions, the firm's own record, and the open web for experts in the field you name, with the rulings that admitted or excluded their testimony, and a memo for the lawyer. The lawyer vets every name.

Workflows built around your firm

Ninety-nine workflows today, from intake to appeal, tuned to your own filings during onboarding and corrected by your lawyers, not ours. The paper gets done. You keep the strategy.

Whole folders, whole dockets

Drop in a whole folder. Hundreds of documents land in the matter, read, OCR'd, and filed where they belong. Drop in the docket: every filing lands in order, every date is offered to the calendar for a person to confirm.

New filings as they happen

New filings are captured as the court sends them. The notice lands in the matter within minutes, the dates are pulled, and the team on the matter is told.

The conference room

Sit down with the desks on a case: senior counsel, head counsel, the scrub, the librarian, the record keeper, each able to ask the others. What they offer is a suggestion. The attorney of record always has the final word.

Drafts that land, authority that is checked

The draft lands on the desk that asked for it, open in Word. Every authority in it is checked by a second mind against the library, and anything it cannot verify is marked for a person. No citation goes out on an agent's word.

Bulk drafting

List the matters, say what each one needs, press once. Every draft files to its own matter and lands for review, with a Jobs line showing where each is.

Your phones, inside the build

Every call is captured on the matter, so the finer points of a client's words are never lost. With the client's permission, the conversation is transcribed and read by the desks.

Also inside: exhibit sets with surgical excerpts, privilege checks, and a number on every page; a trial notebook; a client portal and share links with no login and no Dropbox; engagement letters and declarations by wizard; Word inside the build; security and audit desks; prebills, deposits applied to the right matter, and a trust ledger that ties out, handled by people; Teams and Office 365 where your staff already live.
